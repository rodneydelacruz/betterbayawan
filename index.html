Brief History of Bayawan City

1751

Spanish friars first established a settlement in the area now known as Bayawan, beginning a rich history spanning more than two and a half centuries.

1872

The settlement, earlier known as Tolong Nuevo (part of Tolong Viejo, now the Municipality of Santa Catalina), was formally organized as a pueblo. Its early leaders were the Presidente Municipals, beginning with Estanislao Dumat–ol in 1904.

1953

The name Tolong Nuevo was changed to Bayawan by virtue of Republic Act 694. The name is derived from the Visayan word bayaw, meaning "to hoist" or "to elevate".

1982

During the term of Mayor Felix Gaudiel Jr., the Bayawan Water District was created, ensuring potable water service for the growing community.

Dec 23, 2000

Through a referendum, Bayawan was converted into a component city of Negros Oriental — the largest local government unit in the province with a land area of 69,908 hectares.

2024

The City College of Bayawan was established, and the City Council approved the construction of a Php 800-million New Public Market Complex. Negros Island Region (NIR) was also re-established, uniting Negros Oriental and Occidental under one region.

Dec 22, 2025

Bayawan City celebrated its 25th Silver Anniversary (Handum), marking two and a half decades of progress as a component city of Negros Oriental.

Agricultural Capital

Known as the Agricultural Capital of Negros Oriental, about 97.7% of the city's 69,908 hectares is rural and basically agricultural. Major products include sugarcane, rice, corn and coconut, complemented by a 15-kilometer coastline rich in coastal resources.

Origin of the Name

Bayawan comes from the Visayan word bayaw — "to hoist" or "to elevate". Another local tradition holds that the area was once submerged underwater before rising into fertile, productive land.
